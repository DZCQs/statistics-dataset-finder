# Statistics catalog discovery: 2026-11-26

## Discovery and screening

The API pass collected **1,066 raw entries**, **975 unique leads**, and **859 leads not matched by the automated title/DOI check**. These counts cover paper and repository leads, not verified paper-data pairs. The journal pass screened another 30 entries from [AEJ: Applied Economics](https://www.aeaweb.org/issues/862) and [AEJ: Economic Policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/issues/866); those are reported separately because they were not deduplicated against the API pool.

The search rotated through imputation and nonignorable missingness, Bayesian computation and MCMC diagnostics, causal and quasi-experimental methods, and sparse or high-dimensional modeling. Verification used publisher statements and supplements, arXiv, CRAN documentation and package data, author repositories and data pages, OpenICPSR, the World Bank Microdata Library, UCI, GroupLens, and CDC.

The targeted pass documented **32 detailed reviews**: **25 with clear data sources**, of which **23 were added** and two were already cataloged, and seven held. The pass stopped below 50 because only verified paper-to-data matches were eligible. Where verification used file inventories, no download is implied. Where a paper mixed public and restricted materials, only the identifiable public subset or documented, terms-governed access route was cataloged. Analysis code was not executed.

## Whole-catalog label health

Ran `relabel-catalog.mjs` and then `assess-labels.mjs`. The relabeler assigned the existing `probabilistic programming` label to Bayes-Raking, whose repository supplies reusable Stan model files. The assessor found **82 active official labels** across **2,347 papers**, with no unknown labels, underused labels, registry hierarchy issues, or unflagged label-count violations. No official or candidate label was added, changed, or promoted; `LABEL_CANDIDATES` is empty. All 23 records are marked `auto-discovered` and `needs-review`; four also carry `needs-label-review`.

The largest gains were causal inference and treatment effect estimation (+10 each), missing data (+7), Bayesian inference and quasi-experimental designs (+6 each), and randomized experiments and MCMC diagnostics (+4 each). Examples include store-price and effective-tax-rate panels, waterbird counts, ozone and storm-surge series, and matrix-valued EEG. The 23 new records cover 30 labels and include their required parents.

The direct-assignment audit found **1,021 nonempty label pairs**, up from 1,016. Four existing normalized-title groups with duplicates, 14 zero-topic records, and 323 missing direct-parent assignments across 313 older records are unchanged. The strongest overlap remains survival models and survival analysis (Jaccard 0.97); that pair adds little discrimination as a direct-label intersection. Causal inference and its treatment effect estimation branch increased from 241 to 251 shared records.
